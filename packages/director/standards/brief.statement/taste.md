# brief.statement (director)

Taste criteria for the critic (R15.4): scored 1 to 5, never pass or fail.

## decisive

- 1: Lists options or restates the question; a team could not act on it.
- 2: Leans one way but hedges every answer with may, could or depending.
- 3: Chooses, but one answer is vague enough to read two ways.
- 4: Every answer is a choice a team could act on; one wording still softens it.
- 5: Every answer is one plain choice a team could act on tomorrow, with nothing to reinterpret.

## evidence-weight

- 1: The Because lines restate the direction or cite nothing that decides it.
- 2: Reasons are generic (market trends, best practice) and would back any answer.
- 3: Reasons are specific but one of them would not change the answer if it were false.
- 4: Each reason is specific and decisive; the strongest is not first.
- 5: The deciding reason leads, each line would flip the answer if false, and nothing is padding.

## tripwire-usefulness

- 1: The watch is a wish (keep an eye on growth) with no threshold that matters.
- 2: A threshold exists but crossing it would not change any decision.
- 3: Threshold and action are real but the window is too long to catch a wrong call early.
- 4: Threshold, window and action are real; the action is slightly vague about who acts.
- 5: Crossing the line would plainly reverse or revisit this direction, early enough to matter.

## memorable

- 1: A paragraph of qualifiers nobody could repeat.
- 2: Accurate but long; the point is in the second half.
- 3: Repeatable after a second reading.
- 4: Repeatable after one reading; one word is jargon.
- 5: One plain sentence a new hire would repeat correctly after hearing it once.
